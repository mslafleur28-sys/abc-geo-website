Why GEO is overtaking traditional SEO

Answer-first

Search is shifting from ten blue links to synthesized answers. GEO optimizes for extraction and citation inside ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and peers—not just ranking pages.

Classic SEO rewarded keywords, backlinks, and click-through. Generative engines reward clarity of entity, explicit action, and structured proof. When an answer model needs a source, it prefers pages that declare what something is and what it does in unambiguous, parseable form.

abcGEO treats that shift as a product opportunity: publish GEO editorial that models the pattern, and ship tools whose primary action is already formatted for citation.

How AI crawlers parse structured entities

Answer-first

AI crawlers map pages to entities, attach transitive actions, and lift short authoritative spans—especially when schema and A + B tags reinforce the same story.

Modern retrieval pipelines do more than tokenize text. They:

  • Identify named entities (brands, tools, frameworks)
  • Bind verbs that describe what those entities do
  • Extract compact “answer spans” suitable for quotation
  • Cross-check structured data (JSON-LD, microdata) against visible copy

When your page says “INSTASTACK Generates standardized stack configs” in prose and in schema, you reduce ambiguity. That consistency is the operational heart of A + B = GEO.

Applying A + B = GEO to content

Answer-first

Lead every section with a citable answer that names the entity, states the verb, and delivers the GEO output in one or two sentences.

Editorial rules abcGEO uses across the blog stream:

  • Answer-first boxes under every H2—short, extractable, entity-anchored
  • Entity continuity—repeat the core subject (abcGEO, INSTASTACK, GEO) without synonym fog
  • Verb precision—prefer transitive verbs that encode function (Generates, Standardizes, Configures)
  • Schema alignment—FAQPage and SoftwareApplication markup that mirrors on-page claims

Case study: How Daikin executed A + B = GEO

Answer-first

Daikin Industries—the world’s largest HVAC manufacturer—restructured its digital footprint with entity infrastructure (A) and modular, action-tied content (B) so ChatGPT and Perplexity could cite the brand on category queries.

If a $33B global giant with roughly 15% of the worldwide HVAC market needs to re-engineer its content for AI search engines, what does that mean for your brand? Here is how Daikin used the A + B = GEO formula.

Daikin operates in more than 170 countries, controls approximately 1,344 subsidiary companies, and employs 104,095 people across 324 consolidated subsidiaries. Trailing twelve-month revenue reached $33.3 billion (group net sales surpassing 5 trillion yen). The company holds the number-one global HVAC market share (about 14–16%), ranks #399 on the Forbes Global 2000 by sales, and runs the Daikin Texas Technology Park—a 4.1-million-square-foot facility that is the world’s largest HVAC manufacturing plant. In North America, Daikin also holds stadium naming rights for the Houston Astros’ MLB ballpark (Daikin Park).

Scale alone was not enough for generative engines. Daikin was already strong in classic Google SEO, yet LLMs were not pulling the brand into conversational summaries because content was trapped in dense, unreadable text blocks. The team restructured that footprint using the exact logic of A + B = GEO.

Variable A — The entity: establishing clarity

Problem: Daikin was a massive, unambiguous brand to humans and classic search—but content density blocked LLM extraction. Pronoun-heavy copy (“we,” “our”) also weakens entity tracking across long context windows.

Action: The optimization team built entity infrastructure: strict HTML hierarchies, clear schema markup, and centralized brand identifiers so AI engines could index Daikin as a definitive, unambiguous entity node.

Where to establish A on a website:

  • About Us — primary entity node; the first sentence should firmly name the organization
  • Homepage hero — headline or subheadline should name the entity alongside its core identity
  • Boilerplate / footer — a consistent, concise entity definition on every page for crawlers
  • Leadership & trust pages — executives as extensions of the main entity to reinforce topical authority

Variable B — The transitive verb: defining the market action

Problem: Traditional SEO targeted broad phrases like “energy-efficient HVAC.” In generative space, LLMs do not only match keywords—they evaluate which brand performs an action.

Action: Daikin abandoned generic paragraphs and rebuilt pages into modular, answer-extractable formats. Copy explicitly tied the brand entity to high-intent problem-solving actions—for example, explaining how a Daikin system regulates specific climate zones or calculates heat-pump pricing structures.

Where to map B on a website:

  • Product & service pages — connect entity and action to the offering. Weak: “Our products are high quality.” Optimized: “Daikin manufactures residential and commercial HVAC systems with zone-level climate control.”
  • Press releases & company history — AI models pull milestones for timeline queries. Optimized: “Daikin acquired McQuay International to expand its commercial HVAC footprint.”
  • Case studies & impact reports — prove the transitive action with hard data. Optimized: “Daikin manages an integrated production system covering material sourcing through final delivery.”

The result — cite-worthy GEO content

Answer-first

Because Daikin paired Entity (A) cleanly with transitive Verb Action (B), AI engines scraped modular topics and shifted the brand from “known but invisible” to a frequently cited reference in ChatGPT and Perplexity consumer answers.

The direct object of the transitive verb is the payload the AI wants to deliver. To be citation-worthy, that result must be factual, specific, and ideally quantifiable. Audit marketing fluff that breaks the formula—for example, “Innovation is at the heart of our manufacturing process” cannot be extracted as a concrete fact. Rewrite to satisfy A + B = GEO:

Daikin [A] established [B] an ISO 9001-certified quality management system to ensure products meet international standards [GEO].
