1. Why Daikin’s Scale Still Wasn’t Enough

Daikin Industries is the world’s largest manufacturer of heating, ventilation, and air-conditioning (HVAC) equipment. The company operates in more than 170 countries and controls approximately 1,344 subsidiary companies globally. Trailing twelve-month revenue reached $33.3 billion, with group net sales surpassing 5 trillion yen. Daikin holds the number-one spot in global HVAC market share—about 14% to 16% of the entire industry—employs 104,095 people across 324 consolidated subsidiaries, and ranks #399 in sales on the Forbes Global 2000.

The physical footprint matches the financial one. The Daikin Texas Technology Park outside Houston spans 4.1 million square feet, making it the world’s largest HVAC manufacturing plant and the fifth-largest factory of any kind globally. In North America, Daikin owns stadium naming rights to the Houston Astros’ MLB ballpark—officially Daikin Park.

If a brand of that magnitude still needed to restructure content for generative engines, the lesson for mid-market and emerging brands is unambiguous: classic organic rankings and brand awareness do not automatically translate into AI citations.

2. The Recognition–Citation Gap

A landmark Q2 2026 study by Victorious SEO found that AI engines accurately recognize 96% of brands, but mention or cite almost none—only 11%—in response to category queries. That gap is the operational problem Generative Engine Optimization exists to solve.

Daikin already ranked strongly in organic Google results. LLMs, however, were not pulling the brand into conversational summaries because content was trapped in dense, unreadable text blocks. Knowledge of the brand was not the same as extractable, cite-worthy proof of what Daikin does.

To bridge the gap, Daikin restructured its digital footprint using the exact logic of abcGEO’s A + B = GEO formula: establish the entity, bind transitive actions, and ship modular objects engines can quote.

3. Variable A: Establishing Entity Clarity

For a generative engine to confidently cite a result, it needs to know exactly who is performing the action. A common traditional SEO mistake is relying too heavily on pronouns—“we,” “our,” “us”—which AI can lose track of in long context windows. The A variable must be anchored explicitly.

Problem: Daikin was already a definitive global brand, but crawlers could not reliably treat page copy as a clean entity node when identity was buried in marketing paragraphs.

Action: The optimization team built entity infrastructure. They used strict HTML hierarchies, clear schema markup, and centralized brand identifiers across the web so AI engines could instantly index Daikin as a definitive, unambiguous entity node.

Primary surfaces for A: the About Us page (first sentence names the organization), homepage hero copy (entity + core identity), footer boilerplate (consistent definition on every URL), and leadership/trust pages (executives as extensions of the main entity).

4. Variable B: Defining the Market Action

Generative engines do not only want to know that Daikin exists—they want to know what Daikin does. Transitive verbs require a direct object (your GEO result). Strong, active verbs—manufactures, developed, acquired, reduced, eliminated—create highly extractable facts.

Problem: Traditional SEO targeted broad phrases like “energy-efficient HVAC.” In generative engine space, LLMs evaluate which brand performs an action, not which page repeats a keyword cluster.

Action: Daikin abandoned generic paragraphs and rebuilt pages into highly modular, answer-extractable formats. Copy explicitly tied the brand entity to high-intent problem-solving actions—for example, explaining precisely how a Daikin system regulates specific climate zones or calculates complex heat-pump pricing structures.

5. The Result: Cite-Worthy GEO Content

Because the content paired the Entity (A) cleanly with the Transitive Verb Action (B), AI engines easily scraped the modular topics. Daikin shifted from being an invisible brand that the AI merely “knew” to becoming a primary, highly visible reference frequently cited in ChatGPT and Perplexity results for consumer queries.

The direct object of your transitive verb is the payload the AI wants to deliver. To be citation-worthy, that result must be factual, specific, and ideally quantifiable—milestones, certifications, capacities, acquisitions, and measurable outcomes—not slogans.

6. Where to Identify and Optimize A and B on Your Website

You do not need Daikin’s manufacturing scale to apply the same audit. Walk the site looking for marketing fluff that breaks the formula, then rewrite sentences so Entity, Verb, and Object are all explicit.

Map A — The entity

  • About Us: Primary entity node; open with a firm definition of who performs the work.
  • Homepage hero: Name the entity beside its core identity in the headline or subheadline.
  • Footer / boilerplate: A concise entity definition on every page for global crawler reinforcement.
  • Leadership & trust: Treat executives as extensions of the main entity to build topical authority.

Map B — The transitive verb

  • Product & service pages: Weak — “Our products are high quality.” Optimized — “Daikin manufactures residential and commercial HVAC systems with zone-level climate control.”
  • Press & history timelines: Optimized — “Daikin acquired McQuay International to expand its commercial HVAC footprint.”
  • Case studies & impact reports: Optimized — “Daikin manages an integrated production system covering material sourcing through final delivery.”

Putting the formula into practice is a rewrite discipline: replace pronouns with the brand name, replace soft verbs with transitive actions, and replace metaphors with objects an answer engine can quote. That is how A + B = GEO moves a brand from recognized to cited—whether you run a $33B HVAC network or a focused product site.
